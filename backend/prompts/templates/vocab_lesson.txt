You are creating a vocabulary mini-lesson for an intermediate learner of
{language}, to review before watching a specific video.

The learner sees very little on screen: the sentence in English, a button to
hear it in {language}, and a few bullet points. Write for that.

For each of the following confirmed vocabulary items, produce a lesson entry
with:

- term

- notes: 2 to 4 bullet points — "things to notice". This is the ONLY
  explanation the learner gets, so it has to earn every line.
    * Each bullet is ONE observation, one sentence, plain language.
    * Say what to NOTICE, not what it is called. Never name a grammatical
      category as if that were the explanation.
        Right: "Putting [[ahí]] at the front puts the emphasis on the place."
        Right: "The [[se]] says the thing falls by itself — nobody drops it."
        Right: "[[la estrella]] is the subject even though it comes after the
                verb, not before."
        Wrong: "A near-future built from [[ir a]] plus the reflexive
                infinitive [[caerse]], with locative fronting."
    * Do NOT explain words an intermediate learner already knows. If the only
      thing to say about a word is its dictionary meaning, leave it out.
    * Skip a bullet rather than pad. Two good ones beat four.
    * Wrap every {language} word or phrase in [[double square brackets]].
      Each is spoken by a native {language} voice while the English around it
      is spoken by an English voice, so an unmarked {language} word gets an
      English accent. Mark the {language} only, never its English gloss:
        Right: The [[se]] says it falls by itself.
        Wrong: The [[se says it falls by itself]].

- example_sentences: exactly 2 examples, each {target, english}, in a
  DIFFERENT situation from the video and from each other. The learner already
  gets the video's own sentence, so these exist to show the pattern is general.
  Same natural/colloquial register as the video. Keep them short.

- video_usage: the exact sentence from the transcript where the term appears,
  as {target_sentence, english_translation, timestamp_seconds}.

Items are labelled [phrase] or [construction] where they are not a single
word. A construction is a chunk whose individual words the learner already
knows — what defeats them is parsing it at speed, so the notes should say
which part to listen for and why it does not sound like the textbook order.

Context about the video (use for accuracy, not verbatim insertion):
Description: {description}

Output ONLY valid JSON: an array of objects with keys term, notes (array of
strings), example_sentences (array of {target, english}), video_usage
({target_sentence, english_translation, timestamp_seconds}). No other text.
